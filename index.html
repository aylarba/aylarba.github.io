01 · About

Background

I started out in chemistry at Shiraz University, where quantum chemistry was what first pulled me in. I loved that it was so unknown and so abstract, working with things you could never directly see. Then COVID hit, and in the middle of everything I found myself drawn to biology, AI, and math instead. That's where I landed on bioinformatics, and I went on to get my Master's in it, curious about how it applied to clinical trials and how rewarding that kind of work could be.

During my Master's, alongside the computational work, I also kept and maintained a mouse colony, hands-on wet-lab work that gave me a different kind of respect for where the data actually comes from.

After graduating, I started out as a scientist analyzing data. About a year in, I moved toward the engineering side of the work, contributing to the pipelines and infrastructure that make that analysis possible at scale, which is where I've stayed since.

Today, that means developing and contributing to NGS analysis pipelines, including RNA-seq, WGS/WES, and DNA methylation, across large harmonized pediatric cancer cohorts, alongside HLA typing and neoantigen prediction work. I work with CWL workflows, run compute on AWS and CAVATICA/Seven Bridges, and work across HPC environments in Python, R, and Bash.

What excites me most right now is where engineering and AI agents are heading. There's real room to build, and I think a creative mind, critical thinking, and systems thinking matter more than ever because of it. What I'm working toward is the next step in that same shift: moving from contributing to pipelines to building them from the ground up, taking the engineering edge and systems thinking I've developed inside genomics and applying it more broadly, wherever complex, multidisciplinary problems need someone who can design the infrastructure underneath them.
